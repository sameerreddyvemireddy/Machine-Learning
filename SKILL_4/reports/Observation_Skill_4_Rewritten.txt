Observation – Skill 4: Linear Regression and Regularisation

In this experiment, I used the One Piece bounty dataset to study linear regression and regularisation techniques. The target variable was converted to log10 bounty, and the model used rank and name-related features. After setting aside entries with unknown bounties and excluding the specified outliers and yen-denominated bounty, 142 rows were used.

Polynomial features of degree 2 were selected through cross-validation. I compared OLS, Ridge, Lasso and ElasticNet. OLS performed well on training data but its test score dropped, indicating overfitting. Regularised models improved generalisation. ElasticNet gave a test R² of 0.956 and a test RMSE of 0.140 in log10 units, with seven non-zero coefficients. Lasso achieved a similar test score and also retained seven coefficients, while Ridge reduced coefficient sizes without setting them exactly to zero.

The ablation results showed that rank was much more predictive than name-only clues. However, rank is derived from bounty ordering, so it can leak information about the target and should not be treated as an independent real-world predictor. The residual plots and model-comparison charts helped assess prediction quality and model behaviour. Overall, regularisation controlled the polynomial model's complexity, but the rank leakage is an important limitation when interpreting the results.
